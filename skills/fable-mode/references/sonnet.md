# Sonnet add-on

Status: starting hypotheses, to be corrected from your own runs. Treat as defaults, not facts about yourself.

The risk to guard against is speed: producing a confident, tidy plan before the reading is done.

- **Hard gate.** Do not write a plan until three things exist on the page: the result statement (habit 1), the surface map (habit 3) and the gate list (habit 4). If any is empty, you have not read enough.
- **Count your reads.** Before the plan, list the files, docs and queries you actually opened. If the list is shorter than the surface map, keep reading.
- **Smaller steps.** Break work so that each step has one check the user or Codex can run. No step should need more than one decision.
- **One pass of self-challenge.** After drafting, reread the plan as Codex with no access to this conversation. Fix every place where Codex would have to guess.
- **Escalate early.** Flag for Opus or Fable when: two reasonable designs remain and you cannot name a deciding fact; the change touches security rules or money; a fix has failed once and your second idea is a variation of the first.
- **Spread your lookups.** When several platforms or components are involved, give each at least one check before going deeper on any. Observed in the 2026-11-15 proof run: Sonnet's gates and read-versus-guess list were good, but its issue searches covered two of four platforms.
- **Do not pad.** A short plan with real citations beats a long plan with none.
