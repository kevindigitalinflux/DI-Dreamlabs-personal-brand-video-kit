# Personal-brand content craft

Adapted from Ootto's MIT-licensed claude-content-skills (https://github.com/Ootto-AI/claude-content-skills; licence text in `UPSTREAM-LICENSE-Ootto-MIT.txt`). Rewritten, not copied verbatim. Voice, audience and rules come from `brand/brand.json`.

## Ground rules

1. Voice: first person, warm, direct, plain words. No guru energy, no hype, no jargon. Show, do not credential.
2. Never invent numbers, results, client names or reach. Every claim must be the owner's true story or something they can show on screen. Upstream view counts are one account's data: treat them as hypotheses.
3. One audience and one call to action per piece.
4. Review-before-send. This skill drafts. The owner approves anything posted, scheduled, replied or sent.
5. A keyword call to action ("comment X") only when the owner can deliver the promised thing. Otherwise "link in bio" or "follow".
6. Never copy another creator's hook word for word. Model the technique, write new words.
7. Pay, income, health and results claims: plain, sourced, with a footer that says what the figures are and are not.

## 1. Hooks

The first 3 seconds decide the video. Ask who it is for and what they should feel or do. Write 10 hooks across these angles: bold or contrarian claim; "if you [situation], do this"; a mistake ("stop doing X"); number plus payoff; curiosity gap; result first; a question that names the pain. Under 12 words, spoken language, plus a 3 to 5 word on-screen version. Mark the 3 to test first, with one line why.

Checklist (every pick must pass):
1. Frame 0 already shows something, no black frame, no slow fade, no logo-only card.
2. Frame 0 shows something the viewer recognises.
3. Something happens before 3 seconds.
4. The claim lands in the first 1.2 to 2 seconds and reads on mute.
5. The first spoken word is in the first 0.5 s and is the subject ("Hey guys" is a flop pattern).
6. It speaks to the viewer's own situation ("you", "your").
7. The real thing appears fast: the actual screen, not a stock backdrop.

A/B tests: two hooks that pull different levers, everything else equal, read 3-second retention, not likes. Say so if the sample is too small to mean anything.

## 2. Scripts (30 to 60 s)

Hook (0 to 3 s) restating the promise; context (3 to 8 s) the problem or stakes; value (8 to 45 s) three fast concrete beats; payoff plus one call to action (last 5 s). About 140 to 170 spoken words. Output two columns, spoken line and on-screen text, plus one line of B-roll or screen action per beat and two alternate calls to action. Read aloud and cut 20 percent. Script the hook and call to action BEFORE recording, and note the words that must be said exactly.

## 3. On-screen text

One line per beat, the headline of that beat, not the transcript. Mark the single keyword to emphasise. Tie each line to the spoken word it appears on. Flag any line over about 7 words. Mute pass: read the lines alone; if the video makes no sense, rewrite.

## 4. Call to action

One ask matched to the video. Write the reason line first, then the exact spoken and on-screen words, then placement. State what the owner must have ready if they promise something.

## 5. Repurposing

From one long source pull the 5 most clip-worthy ideas (surprising claim, how-to, myth, story, result), each with a hook and a 3-beat outline, and flag the best 2 with a reason. Everything must come from the source.

## Output format for a plan

WHO IT IS FOR / HOOK (0 to 3 s) / CHECKLIST / SCRIPT (line | on screen) / CALL TO ACTION and what delivers it / CAPTION. Flag every assumption and every claim the owner must confirm as true.
